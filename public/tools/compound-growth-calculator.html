Compound growth calculator

Enter a starting balance, a monthly contribution, an annual return, the number of years and how often returns compound. The result shows the final balance, what you contributed and the interest earned.

How it works

Each period the balance grows by the periodic rate, and returns then earn returns themselves. With annual compounding the balance is start x (1 + rate) ^ years.

Example

Without contributions, $10,000 at 7% a year for 10 years becomes $10,000 x 1.07^10, about $19,672.

Questions

Why does a longer time matter so much?

Growth is exponential, so later years add far more than early ones.

Are returns guaranteed?

No. Real returns vary from year to year and can be negative.
